About CineStream

CineStream is a movie and television discovery platform, and a small library of public domain films you can watch in full. It is built and run by VAF Ubwenge Tech, a software company based in Kigali, Rwanda.

What the site is for

Most people decide what to watch with far less information than they would like. Ratings are reduced to a single number, summaries are written to sell rather than to inform, and the question of whether something is actually worth two hours of an evening is usually answered by a poster. CineStream exists to make that decision better informed: every title carries a full synopsis, cast and crew credits, a rating shown alongside its vote count, and written reviews so you can see the reasoning behind the score rather than only the score.

Alongside that browsing catalogue, we maintain a smaller library of films you can watch here in full. These are public domain titles, verified against a public archive, and they are the only films on which CineStream offers complete playback. The distinction is explained in detail on our disclaimer page and throughout our blog.

Who builds it

VAF Ubwenge Tech is a student-led technology company working across web, mobile and applied AI. Our focus is on practical software with a particular interest in problems relevant to Rwanda and the wider region, including language technology for Kinyarwanda. CineStream is our public showcase of frontend engineering, third-party API integration and interface design, and it is maintained as a working product rather than as a demonstration.

How the site is built

The interface is a React and TypeScript application styled with Tailwind CSS and built with Vite. Accounts, saved lists and watch progress are handled through Firebase Authentication and Firestore. Film and television metadata comes from The Movie Database. Full-length public domain playback is served from the Internet Archive's own player, and trailers play through YouTube's official embed. We do not host video files ourselves.

How it is funded

CineStream is free to use and carries advertising. Ad placements are labelled and kept out of the way of the content they sit beside, and we do not accept paid placement inside editorial writing or rankings. Nothing in the trending rows, genre rows, blog articles or ratings is influenced by an advertiser. Our privacy policy describes what advertising partners collect and how to opt out of personalised ads.

Editorial policy

Every article on our blog is written by the CineStream team. We do not publish syndicated, spun, or automatically generated articles, and we do not republish other sites' content. Where an article describes how a part of the site works, it describes the behaviour as actually implemented; where it covers film history or technique, it is written from general knowledge of the subject rather than copied from a source. If you find something inaccurate, we would genuinely like to know.

What we will not do

  • Host, mirror, or distribute copyrighted films.
  • Embed video from unlicensed third-party streaming hosts to pad out the catalogue.
  • Link out to sites that distribute infringing copies.
  • Disguise advertising as site content or as a play button.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about how CineStream works, what you can watch, and why the playable library is smaller than the catalogue. If your question is not here, write to us.

Is CineStream free?

Yes. There is no subscription, no trial and no payment of any kind. The site is funded by advertising, which is labelled and kept separate from content. You do not need an account to browse the catalogue, read articles, watch trailers or watch the public domain films.

Why can I not watch most films in full?

Because we only stream films that are in the public domain. For any title still under copyright, CineStream shows information only: synopsis, cast, ratings, reviews and a trailer where one exists. We do not host copyrighted films and do not embed unlicensed third-party video sources to fill the gap. That keeps the playable library small and the site legal, which is a trade-off we have made deliberately.

What does public domain mean?

It means the copyright on a work has ended, so no one holds exclusive rights over it and anyone may copy, share or watch it freely. Films reach that state when their copyright term expires, when a required renewal was never filed, or when they were published without a valid copyright notice under older rules. Our article on what public domain means covers this in detail.

Where do the films actually play from?

Full-length public domain films play through the Internet Archive's own embedded player. Trailers play through YouTube's official embed. CineStream stores no video files and streams nothing from its own servers.

Where does the movie information come from?

Titles, posters, synopses, cast lists, runtimes and ratings come from The Movie Database (TMDB), a community-maintained film and television database. If you find an error in a record, correcting it at TMDB fixes it everywhere, including here.

Why does a film have no poster or a very short description?

Because the source record is incomplete. Coverage at TMDB is excellent for well-known titles and thin for obscure, older or non-English ones. A placeholder image is a gap in the data rather than a fault on the page.

Do I need an account?

Only for two features: My List, which saves titles you want to watch later, and Continue Watching, which remembers where you stopped. Everything else works signed out. Sign-in is handled by Google, so we never see your password.

What is the difference between My List and Continue Watching?

My List is manual and records intent: nothing appears there unless you add it. Continue Watching is automatic and records behaviour: any title you start playing appears there, sorted by most recent and capped at your last twenty. One is a shelf you curate, the other is a record of what you actually watched.

Can I use my account on more than one device?

Yes. Your list and watch progress are stored against your account rather than your browser, so signing in on another device brings both with you. That is also why signing out makes the Continue Watching row disappear.

How do I delete my account and data?

Email us from the address your account uses and ask. We will remove your stored profile, saved list and watch history. Our privacy policy explains exactly what is held and for how long.

Why does the quality of some films look poor?

Because the best surviving copy is often poor. Public domain films have no commercial rights holder, so nobody funds a restoration, and many archived copies have been duplicated from already worn prints. A faster connection cannot add detail that was never in the source. Trailers look better because they are modern digital files.

Are there subtitles?

Only where the archived copy already carries them, which for older material is often not the case. We play films through an external archive's player and cannot add text tracks to material we do not host. Silent films are an exception, since their intertitles are part of the image.

How is the trending row calculated?

From a mix of recent release buzz, whether a title's rating is rising or falling, and a cap on how much any one genre can dominate the row. It is recalculated continuously rather than copied from an external chart, so it shifts from day to day.

Why do my search filters reset when I switch tabs?

Deliberately. Genre distributions and typical rating ranges differ between films and series, so a filter set that produces a good shortlist of films often produces an empty or misleading one when applied unchanged to television.

Is CineStream legal?

Yes. We stream only public domain titles verified against a public archive, embed trailers through YouTube's official player, and display licensed metadata with the required attribution. We host nothing and link to no infringing source. Our disclaimer page sets out the copyright position and the takedown procedure in full.

I hold rights in a film you have marked playable. What do I do?

Email us with the title and your claim, following the steps on our disclaimer and DMCA page. We suspend playback for a disputed title while we review the notice rather than after, and we aim to acknowledge every notice within two business days.

Why do I see advertising?

Advertising pays for the site so it can stay free. Ads are labelled and placed between sections rather than disguised as content or as play buttons. Nothing in our rankings, ratings or articles is influenced by an advertiser, and you can opt out of personalised advertising through the links in our cookie policy.

Who makes CineStream?
